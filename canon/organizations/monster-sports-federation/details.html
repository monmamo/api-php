Executive Committee

The Executive Committee (EC) makes the decisions. The EC has the power to create or dissolve offices underneath it to govern various aspects of the sport.

  • Commissioner: The chief executive of the league.
  • Secretary: Oversees the records, files and communications of the league. Takes the minutes and prepares the agendas of EC meetings and other important meetings. Prepares press releases and conferences. Oversees the Offices of Public Relations and Media Relations.
  • Treasurer: Oversees the finances, reserves and assets of the league. Is responsible for compiling and submitting financial reports.
  • Trustees: Four persons who are elected by the clubs to represent the interests of the clubs.

The Trustees are ranked by length of service, and the longer-serving Trustees get tiebreaking privileges as needed. The Commissioner, Secretary, Treasurer are elected by the other members of the Executive Committee.

Leagues and Circuits

The professional leagues operate on the concept of promotion and relegation. The top performing trainers at each level are promoted to the next higher level, while the worst-performing trainers are relegated to the lower level.

Trans-Pluralis League

The first monster sports league organized and sanctioned by the Federation.

Champion League

The top tier of professional monster trainers.

  • Members of the Champion League play for clubs in the largest cities.
  • Trainers win admission into the Champion League by winning the Emerald Tournament.
  • The Champion League operates in a manner very similar to the Elite League, but with a much smaller pool of Trainers. The tiers of the Champion League are named for gemstones. Trainers accumulate victory points in the same way, though the points for Champion League are kept separate from those in the Elite League.
  • At the end of the year, the Champion Trainers compete in the Diamond Tournament, a head-to-head double-elimination tournament. The trainers who lose their first two (and thus only) matches are demoted back to the Elite League.

Elite League

  • Members of the Elite League play for clubs in large cities, including some that do not have Champion trainers.
  • Each season, the Monster Sports Federation promotes trainers from the Master League to fill positions opened in the Elite League by trainers promoted to the Champion League, trainers leaving the Elite League due to retirement, or expansion.
  • MonFed classifies Elite Trainers in a series of tiers, each identified by Medals. The metal levels, from lowest to highest, are thus: Copper, Aluminum, Nickel, Iron, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Titanium. Trainers enter the Elite League in the lowest level.
  • Trainers participate in official League competitions (Challenge, Battle or Race) throughout the season. Most of a trainer's competition will be against a trainer on the same level, though a trainer may compete with someone up to two levels above or below him.
  • The performance of a Trainer is measured with victory points. A Trainer starts his career with zero victory points. He carries these with him through his career; they are not reset at the end of the season. Trainers accumulate victory points by winning competitions. Victory points are awarded or taken away as follows:
    • For defeating a trainer below one's level or at one's level or one level above: one point.
    • For defeating a trainer two levels above: two points.
    • For losing to a trainer at or above one's level or one level below: no points.
    • For losing to a trainer two levels below: one point is deducted.
  • The Elite League season begins in the second month of the year and runs through the 11th month, for a total of 10 months. At the end of each month, all trainers are evenly distributed among the medal-levels according to victory point totals. The trainers with the most points go into the Titanium Tier, the next set go into the Platinum Tier, and so on.
  • Each month, trainers in the Titanium and Platinum tiers are invited to participate in Champion Tournaments. These tournaments are single-elimination one-on-one competitions. The ultimate winner of each tournament receives an invitation to join the Champion League in the following season. The winner of a Champion Tournament may not participate in any other Champion Tournaments at the Elite League level.
  • At the end of the season, a final redistribution takes place. Trainers who are moving on to the Champion League are removed from the pool, and the pool is distributed. The rank that the trainers hold is what they will hold during the first month of the next season. Trainers who are coming into the league will be unranked during the first month of competition, then will be seeded at the end of the first month.

Master League

  • For amateur trainers who have completed all certifications and are deemed most competent to engage in Monster competition.
  • Master League members compete in leagues operated by local clubs.
  • Master League trainers are promoted from their local Certified Trainer League at the discretion of the club running the league.

Certified Trainer League

  • The bottom tier at which participants buy into the league system and are certified as Monster trainers. The Trainer who achieves the rank of Certified Trainer has demonstrated that he is serious about Monster training.
  • Certified Trainer League members compete in leagues operated by local clubs.

Novice League

A league composed of trainers who are not paid but can win cash prizes and admission into Certified Trainer League. Open to any who has a trained Monster and the desire to compete.

College League

A league composed of unpaid trainers who are students at various colleges and universities.

All-Star Circuit

  • A touring showcase of older "all-star" trainers.
  • Rather than compete through the tournament circuit, trainers in the All-Star Circuit INCOMPLETE.
